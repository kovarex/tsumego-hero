Appendix B. How the leaves were checked

  1. The solver, an exact local search, checked every leaf of the status problems in the export of 10 November. On {total} leaves its status differs from the tree's S[]: a different class (alive, dead, ko, bent four) or a different ko sign.
  2. For each problem, an analyst read the disagreeing leaves like a strong player, used the solver as a calculator, and decided whether the tree or the solver is wrong, or whether it is a question of definitions.
  3. Every "the tree is wrong" verdict went to two reviewers who tried to refute it, one by reading the position and one against besogo's definitions. A leaf counts as a clear error only if both agreed. Every "the solver is wrong" verdict went to a reviewer who tried to show the tree wrong.
  4. Where the reviewers split, two independent judges settled the leaf. Each definition question went to a fact checker, who played out the lines it rests on.
  5. besogo's own grading code, the site's tree updater run in a headless browser, applied each fix to its tree and listed the moves that change between correct and wrong. For every problem where some do, a checker tried to show the new grading wrong and named any tree change it needs.
  6. Statuses follow besogo's definitions, with s and o the outside ko threats of the first player and the opponent: KO+ means the first player wins when s ≥ o, KO+2 when s ≥ o − 1, KO− when s ≥ o + 1, KO−2 when s ≥ o + 2. A local move the other side must answer counts as a ko threat.
