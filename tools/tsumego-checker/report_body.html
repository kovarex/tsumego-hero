Tsumego Correctness Checker

How far we can automate finding wrong refutations, missing correct moves and wrong statuses in the site's problems. This version runs on your export of all 12,574 problems and includes the fixes from your reviews.

You confirmed all of these.
  • Status problems are the best test set. Every authored S[] leaf is a test case. On the site's 307 status problems the solver agrees with the author on STATUS_RATE of the leaves it decides (STATUS_AGREE of STATUS_DECIDED); it leaves STATUS_UNDECIDED_SHARE of the leaves undecided.
  • Precision comes first. The checker now reports a finding only when it holds in two independent solves (normal region, then a wider region with 5× the budget) and the problem is proven unsettled. On the status problems that leaves CHECK_FLAGGED flagged problems out of CHECK_DONE, all listed below for your review.
  • Your reviews fixed the solver, not the trees. Of the earlier "findings" you looked at, 1814, 15405 and 7543 were solver errors. Each one exposed a modelling bug, now fixed (list below). The biggest single gain came from your dynamic-region idea: 2.4× more hard positions decided, in half the time.
  • Still weak: many positions stay undecided within the budget, and problems without a stated goal ("Black to play", 6,586 of them) can't be checked yet.

Speed

  • The engine runs about 360,000 nodes per second per core. Caching end-of-line tests (Benson, eye space) by position made it 7.5× faster.
  • On 40 hard leaves (undecided before), the dynamic region decides 15–17 at 300,000 nodes in about 40 s; the static region decided 7 in 82 s.
  • A 5× bigger budget (1,000,000 nodes per ko mode) barely helps: on the status problems the solver decides 60% of the leaves instead of 57%, and agreement goes from 94% to 95%; 20 problems then hit the 2-minute limit. The undecided leaves need a smarter search (move ordering, relevance zones), not more nodes.
  • Your C++ question: on the solver's inner loops PHP 8.3 is 5× slower at liberty counting (3× with JIT) and 30× slower at node expansion with hashing.

How the site's problems work (for reference)

  • Two marking systems. Marker problems: a leaf whose comment starts with + is correct; on the solver's move one correct child is enough, on the opponent's every child must be correct. Status problems: G[KILL]/G[LIVE] on the root and S[] on the leaves; the moves keeping the best status are correct. SKD[] makes super ko count as dead.
  • besogo follows transpositions (a move reaching a position already in the tree) and fails any other move off the tree. The checker does the same.
  • Current version: latest sgf row with sgf_status_id = 2 per problem. Of 12,574 problems, 307 are status-based, 5,371 marker problems state kill or live, 310 say capture, 6,586 only say "Black to play".

Architecture

flowchart LR
  S[SGF version] --> A[A: goal, target, region]
  A --> B[B: df-pn solver C++]
  B <--> C[C: Benson, eye space, ko rules]
  B --> V[Second solve: wider region, 5x budget]
  V --> F[Findings]
  F --> Q[Admin review queue]
  Q --> P[SGF proposal with the fix]
A · goal
  • G[], else the description (kill, live, capture).
  • Target: the largest group of that colour; chains for capture goals.
  • Proven unsettled (solver first wins, opponent first loses) before anything is reported.
  • Region: the defender's area plus the tree's moves, with a dynamic fringe around it.
B · search
  • df-pn with the 1+ε trick; 24-byte table entries with Zobrist keys; transpositions merged.
  • Two ko modes (unlimited threats for either side).
  • Fringe moves only once a neighbouring chain is short of liberties.
C · end positions
  • Benson's unconditional life; a sound "no two eyes possible" test.
  • Game end: a target chain in atari is taken. A cycle through a ko is dead; a cycle of legal captures is a seki.
  • Bent four in the corner: when the game ends with the defender relying on ko threats, the attacker removes them and starts the ko when it likes; if that kills, the status is BENT4. A ko the defender can start itself (1807) stays KO.
  • Not yet: seki vs alive, ten thousand years ko, approach and threat counts for ko (besogo's KO+n, A+n).

Where it runs and how results reach admins

  • Batch: a command-line run over your export on your PC or a VPS (a full run is a few CPU-hours), producing a JSON file of findings for an admin to upload.
  • Editor: the same C++ compiled to WebAssembly, checking an SGF in the uploader's browser with a small budget. No server CPU needed.
  • Queue: sgf_check and sgf_check_finding tables (finding types as a lookup table with constants), an admin page listing open findings with the line, dismiss (kept per SGF version so it doesn't come back) and make proposal (a normal SGF proposal for review).

Plan

  2. Undecided positions: move ordering and initial proof numbers (a small policy net trained on your trees, as you suggested), relevance zones. Goal: under 10% undecided on status problems.
  3. Ko and seki detail: approach count and threat balance of kos, seki in sente vs gote, so status problems can be filled and compared exactly.
